# Image sourcing and rights

## Source preference

Use this order unless the task requires otherwise:

1. User-supplied asset with known ownership.
2. Official product, organization, venue, or brand media kit asset.
3. Public-domain or Creative Commons source with the exact record visible.
4. Commercial-use stock source whose current license explicitly covers the request.
5. Commissioned or generated asset when appropriate and allowed.

Never use a random image search thumbnail as the final source.

## Useful source families

- Wikimedia Commons: strong provenance and license metadata; verify the individual file
  page because a collection can contain multiple licenses.
- Openverse: discovery index; follow through to the original source and license.
- Unsplash and Pexels: check the current license, prohibited uses, and whether
  identifiable people or property create additional risk.
- Government/open-data portals: verify jurisdiction, attribution, and dataset terms.
- Brand media kits: use only supplied assets within the stated trademark and editorial
  rules.

Do not assume that "royalty-free" means "no restrictions", "publicly visible" means
"public domain", or "editorial use" covers marketing.

## License checks

Record separately:

- License name and version.
- Exact license URL.
- Copyright/creator.
- Required attribution text and location.
- Commercial use permission.
- Modification/cropping permission.
- Redistribution permission.
- Editorial-only or no-endorsement restriction.
- Model, property, trademark, or privacy caveat.

Creative Commons is a family, not one permission. CC BY requires attribution; CC BY-SA
has share-alike implications; ND limits derivatives, which can affect cropping; NC
excludes commercial uses. Verify the linked deed for the exact version.

## Query design

Search for the artifact's literal subject, visual state, context, and source:

```text
site:commons.wikimedia.org modern hospital waiting room photograph
```

```text
official press kit electric vehicle charging station product image
```

Add constraints such as indoor/outdoor, overhead/front view, transparent background,
portrait/full body, daylight, no people, or screen close-up only when they affect the
deliverable. Avoid piling contradictory adjectives into one query.

## Visual evaluation

Inspect the finalist at the intended aspect ratio:

- Subject clarity at thumbnail and final size.
- Crop-safe negative space for text or callouts.
- Resolution and compression artifacts.
- Perspective, lighting, and colour consistency with adjacent assets.
- Absence of unintended logos, confidential screens, badges, license plates, or
  identifiable bystanders.
- Whether the composition reflects the real product/place rather than a generic stand-in.

Do not use a heavily blurred image merely because text needs contrast. Recompose or
choose a different asset.
